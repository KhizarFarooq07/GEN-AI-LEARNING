Amsterdam Attractions and Sightseeing

Canals:
Amsterdam's iconic canal system with about 1,500 bridges is the main attraction. Walking or cycling along canals is free. Canal boat tours available: €18-25 for basic tours, €40-60 for dinner cruises.

Anne Frank House:
Former hiding place of Anne Frank during Nazi occupation. Her diary is world-famous. Admission: €14. Very popular; advance booking recommended. Moving and historically significant experience.

Van Gogh Museum:
World's largest collection of Vincent van Gogh's paintings and drawings. Admission: €22. Features interactive exhibits and detailed information about the artist's life and work.

Rijksmuseum:
Netherlands' largest art museum with masterworks including Rembrandt's "Night Watch". Admission: €23. Beautiful building and comprehensive Dutch art collection spanning centuries.

Stedelijk Museum:
Modern and contemporary art museum. Admission: €20. Features international artists and Dutch modern art.

Netherlands Maritime Museum:
Housed in a former admiralty building, features maritime history and interactive exhibits. Admission: €18.

Dam Square:
Central square with the Royal Palace, National Monument, and shops. Entry to square free; palace tours €10.

Vondelpark:
Large urban park with gardens, lake, and cultural venues. Free to enter and explore. Great for cycling and picnicking.

Jordaan District:
Charming neighborhood with narrow streets, galleries, cafés, and independent shops. Walking and exploring free. Gallery admissions vary.

Red Light District (De Wallen):
Historic medieval neighborhood known for window prostitution and cannabis cafés. Walking and exploring free. Many tourists visit for the unique cultural and historical context.

Museum Ons' Lieve Heer op Solder:
Hidden Catholic church built in a private home during periods when Catholics were persecuted. Admission: €12. Fascinating piece of history.

Begijnhof:
One of Amsterdam's oldest neighborhoods featuring preserved 14th-century architecture. Entry free to courtyard.

Bicycle Tours:
Biking is the best way to experience Amsterdam. Bike rentals: €10-15 per day. Guided tours available from €20. City is very bike-friendly.

Flower Markets:
Amsterdam Flower Market (Bloemenmarkt) on Singel canal selling flowers and bulbs. Entry free; flowers priced individually.

Breweries:
Brewery tours and hoppy beer scene. Tours typically €12-20 with beer samples.

Windmills:
Historic windmills still preserved at Kinderdijk (about 15km outside Amsterdam). UNESCO site. Day trip popular with tourists. Train/boat access: €15-25.

Transportation: Amsterdam has trams, buses, and metros. GVB day pass: €8. But biking is preferred by most visitors. Most tourists can navigate the compact city center on foot or by bike.
