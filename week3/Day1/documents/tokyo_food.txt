Japanese Cuisine and Tokyo Food Culture

Japanese cuisine is known for its emphasis on seasonal ingredients, minimal processing, and beautiful presentation. Tokyo offers an incredible variety of Japanese and international foods.

Traditional Japanese Dishes:
- Sushi and Sashimi: Raw fish served with rice or as standalone pieces.
- Ramen: Noodles in a savory broth with toppings like pork, egg, and vegetables.
- Tempura: Battered and deep-fried vegetables and seafood.
- Tonkatsu: Breaded and fried pork cutlet served with sauce and rice.
- Okonomiyaki: Savory pancakes with various fillings.
- Udon: Thick wheat noodles in broth.
- Yakitori: Grilled chicken skewers.
- Donburi: Rice bowls topped with various ingredients like chicken, egg, or seafood.

Food Prices in Tokyo:
Budget options: Convenience store meals (convenience stores like Lawson and 7-Eleven have quality affordable food), 100-yen shops, and simple ramen shops offer meals for ¥500-1500 ($3-10).
Mid-range: Casual restaurants and standing sushi bars offer meals for ¥1500-3000 ($10-20).
Upscale: Fine dining and high-end sushi restaurants charge ¥5000+ ($35+) per person.

Street Food and Markets:
- Tsukiji Outer Market and Toyosu Market offer fresh seafood and prepared foods.
- Street stalls serve takoyaki (octopus balls), okonomiyaki, and other street food.
- Anime and game themed cafés are very popular.

Dining Etiquette:
- Slurping noodles is encouraged and shows appreciation.
- Tipping is not customary in Japan.
- Removing shoes in traditional restaurants is common.

Beverages: Green tea, sake (rice wine), and whisky are traditional drinks. Japanese beer and vending machines offering hot and cold beverages are ubiquitous.

Seasonal Eating: Japanese cuisine emphasizes seasonal ingredients and there are traditional foods associated with each season.
