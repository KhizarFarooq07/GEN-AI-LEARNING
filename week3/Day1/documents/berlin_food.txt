German Cuisine and Berlin Food Culture

German cuisine is characterized by its hearty, filling meals and centuries-old culinary traditions. Berlin, as the capital, features both traditional German dishes and modern international cuisine.

Traditional Berlin Dishes:
- Currywurst: Sliced sausage covered with a spicy curry-ketchup sauce, typically served with French fries. It became popular in Berlin after World War II.
- Döner Kebab: While originally Turkish, it has become a staple food in Berlin and is widely available throughout the city.
- Eintopf: A traditional one-pot stew made with potatoes, vegetables, and meat.
- Schnitzel: Thin breaded and fried meat cutlets, usually served with potatoes and sauce.
- Sauerbraten: A German pot roast made with vinegar.

Food Prices in Berlin:
Budget options: Street food like currywurst can cost €3-6. Kebab sandwiches are €4-8. Simple beer halls offer meals for €8-12.
Mid-range: Traditional restaurants offer meals for €12-25 per person.
Upscale: Fine dining restaurants charge €40+ per person.

Beer Culture: Berlin has a strong beer culture with numerous beer gardens and breweries. Local beers include Berliner Pilsner and various craft beers from modern breweries.

Markets: Berlin's food markets offer fresh produce, international foods, and street food. Notable markets include Markthalle Neun and Street Food Thursday.

Modern Berlin Dining: The city has a booming food scene with trendy restaurants, fusion cuisine, and Michelin-starred establishments. Berlin is known for its vegetarian and vegan food options.
