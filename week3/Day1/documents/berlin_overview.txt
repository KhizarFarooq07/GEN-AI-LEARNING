Berlin - Overview and History

Berlin is the capital and largest city of Germany. With a population of approximately 3.8 million residents, it is the second-largest city in the European Union by population. Berlin is located in northeastern Germany and serves as the political, cultural, and historical center of the country.

History: Berlin was founded in the 13th century and became the capital of the Kingdom of Prussia. The city played a central role in European history, particularly during the 20th century. During World War II, Berlin was heavily damaged. After the war, the city was divided between Soviet, American, British, and French occupation zones. This division eventually led to the construction of the Berlin Wall in 1961, which stood for 28 years and became a symbol of the Cold War. The wall fell on November 9, 1989, leading to the reunification of Germany.

Geography: Berlin is situated on the Spree River and has numerous lakes and parks. The city covers an area of 891 square kilometers and is surrounded by the state of Brandenburg.

Culture: Berlin is known as a vibrant cultural hub with world-class museums, galleries, theaters, and concert halls. The city hosts numerous cultural events and festivals throughout the year and has a thriving contemporary art scene.

Economy: Berlin is an important center for tourism, education, and cultural industries. Major employers include universities, research institutions, and creative industries.
