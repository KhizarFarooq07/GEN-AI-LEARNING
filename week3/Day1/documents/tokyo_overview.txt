Tokyo - Overview and Districts

Tokyo is the capital and most populous metropolitan area of Japan, with over 14 million residents in the city proper and over 37 million in the metropolitan area. It is one of the world's largest and most important metropolitan areas.

History: Tokyo was originally a small settlement called Edo. In 1868, it became the capital of Japan following the Meiji Restoration and industrial revolution. The city was heavily damaged during World War II but was reconstructed and transformed into a modern metropolis. Today, Tokyo is a global center of finance, politics, and culture.

Geography: Tokyo is situated on the eastern coast of Honshu island and is surrounded by the Kanto region. The city is built on relatively flat terrain and is crossed by multiple rivers and canals.

Modern Tokyo: Known for its ultra-modern architecture, technology, fashion, and dynamic energy. The city seamlessly blends traditional Japanese culture with cutting-edge modernity.

Districts:
- Shibuya: Known for shopping, nightlife, and the famous Shibuya Crossing.
- Shinjuku: A major commercial and entertainment hub with towering skyscrapers and nightlife.
- Asakusa: Home to traditional temples and historic neighborhoods.
- Ginza: Upscale shopping district famous for luxury brands.
- Akihabara: Electronics and anime/manga culture hub.
- Harajuku: Youth fashion and street culture.
- Roppongi: Nightlife and entertainment district.

Culture: Tokyo maintains strong ties to traditional Japanese culture while being a center of contemporary innovation, including anime, manga, video games, and fashion.

Population: Incredibly diverse, with significant populations of various nationalities creating very international neighborhoods.

Safety: Tokyo is known as one of the safest cities in the world with low crime rates.

Language: Japanese is the primary language. English signage is common in major areas, but less common in residential neighborhoods.
