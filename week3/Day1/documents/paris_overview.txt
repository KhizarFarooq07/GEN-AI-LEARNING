Paris - Overview and History

Paris is the capital and most populous city of France, located along the Seine River in northern France. With over 2 million residents within the city proper and over 12 million in the metropolitan area, it is one of Europe's largest cities.

History: Paris was founded in the 3rd century with settlements on islands in the Seine. It became the capital of France in the 12th century and developed into one of the most important cities in Europe. During the Renaissance, Paris became a center of learning and culture. The French Revolution, beginning in 1789, was centered in Paris and fundamentally changed European history. The city was occupied during World War II but emerged as a major cultural and political center.

Geography: Paris is situated on the Seine River and is divided into 20 arrondissements (districts), each with distinct character and charm. The city is known for its elegant architecture, tree-lined streets, and bridges.

Culture: Paris is renowned as the City of Light (Ville de la Lumière) and is considered a center of Western European culture and education. The city has influenced art, fashion, literature, and philosophy for centuries.

Language: French is the primary language spoken in Paris. English is widely understood in tourist areas and major establishments.

Climate: Paris has a temperate maritime climate with mild winters and warm summers. Best visiting times are April-June and September-October.

Tourism: Paris is one of the world's top tourist destinations, attracting millions of visitors annually. The city offers world-class museums, iconic monuments, and excellent cuisine.
