Paris Landmarks and Sightseeing

Eiffel Tower:
The most iconic symbol of Paris, built for the 1889 World's Fair. Standing 330 meters tall, it offers panoramic views of the city from various levels. Admission: €10-15 for stairs, €15-20 for elevator. Best visited at sunset.

Louvre Museum:
The world's largest art museum and most visited museum globally. Home to the Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, and thousands of other artworks spanning from ancient civilizations to the 19th century. Admission: €17. The glass pyramid entrance is architecturally iconic.

Notre-Dame Cathedral:
A stunning Gothic cathedral partially damaged by fire in 2019. Currently under restoration but the exterior can be viewed. Free to view from outside. Originally built in the 12th-18th centuries.

Arc de Triomphe:
A monumental arch honoring those who died in French wars. Located at the center of Place Charles de Gaulle, it offers views from the top. Admission: €13. Descending is challenging due to spiral stairs.

Sacré-Cœur Basilica:
Perched atop Montmartre hill, this white Romano-Byzantine basilica offers stunning city views. Interior admission free, dome climb €6. The neighborhood has artistic history and street artists.

Champs-Élysées and Place de la Concorde:
An iconic avenue lined with shops, restaurants, and theaters. Place de la Concorde is a famous public square with historic monuments.

Versailles Palace:
A magnificent royal palace located outside Paris (12 km). Palace admission: €20. Gardens free or €10 for full access. Easily reached by metro or train from central Paris.

Museums and Galleries:
Paris has over 100 museums including Musée d'Orsay (Impressionist art), Picasso Museum, and Centre Pompidou (modern art).

Parks: Luxembourg Gardens, Tuileries Garden, and various parks offer green spaces for relaxation.

Seine River Cruises: Popular evening cruises offer views of illuminated landmarks. Cost: €15-30.

Transportation: Paris has an extensive metro system. Day pass: €8-15.
